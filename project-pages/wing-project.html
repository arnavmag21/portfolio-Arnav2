Booster Rocket Canard Wings and Vertical Stabilizers
The goal of this project is to build a test booster rocket to launch experimenting with design focusing on flight stability and performance.
Project Details / Background
This project focused on the design and fabrication of aerodynamic control surfaces, including wing canards and vertical stabilizers. I worked on developing custom airfoil profiles in SolidWorks and translating those designs from 3D CAD models into manufacturable 2D toolpaths, ensuring both aerodynamic performance and practical manufacturability.
After finalizing the designs, I generated G-code using DevFoam and Mach3 to manufacture the components with a CNC hot-wire foam cutter. A key part of this process was calibrating machine parameters such as feed rate, wire temperature, and axis alignment to achieve accurate cuts while minimizing thermal deformation and preserving airfoil geometry.
To expand manufacturing capability, I also designed and built a custom manual hot-wire cutting apparatus for producing more complex airfoil shapes. This involved constructing a rigid, aligned frame and applying an understanding of heat transfer to optimize cutting performance and prevent material defects such as burn-through or structural weakening.
